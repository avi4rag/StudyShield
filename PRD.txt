# Product Requirements Document (PRD)

## Unacademy Early Warning & Student Retention System

---

## 1. Project Overview

The Unacademy Early Warning & Student Retention System is a simple web application that helps educators identify students who may be losing interest in their studies.

The system checks basic student activity, such as:

- Quiz completion
- Days of inactivity
- Login activity

Based on this information, each student receives a risk score between 0 and 100.

A higher score means the student may need more attention.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

In a large batch, an educator cannot manually check the activity of every student.

For example, some students may:

- Stop logging in
- Miss quizzes or DPPs
- Become inactive for several days

The goal of this project is to automatically identify such students and help educators take action early.

# StudyShield Product Requirements Document

## 1. Product Overview

StudyShield is an educator-focused early-warning and student-retention platform. It combines learner activity and quiz performance into explainable risk signals so educators can identify students who may be disengaging and take action earlier.

The current release is a working prototype focused on the educator monitoring and outreach workflow. It includes a protected dashboard, database-backed student data, risk calculation, filtering, student details, reports, warning signals, and educator nudges.

## 2. Problem Statement

In a large online class, educators cannot manually inspect every student's login activity, quiz completion, missed deadlines, and recent engagement. Students may gradually disengage without directly asking for help.

The problem is therefore both visibility and response:

- Relevant learning data is spread across different records.
- Educators do not have a clear priority list.
- Warning signs are easy to notice too late.
- A detected problem is not automatically connected to a next action.

StudyShield addresses this by turning activity data into a prioritized intervention queue.

## 3. Product Goals

### 3.1 Current goals

The current prototype must:

1. Authenticate educators securely.
2. Store students, batches, quizzes, quiz attempts, activity, users, and nudges.
3. Calculate an explainable risk score from quiz completion and login recency.
4. Display cohort-level metrics and risk distribution.
5. Allow educators to search, filter, and sort students.
6. Show the evidence behind an individual student's score.
7. Allow educators to send and review outreach nudges.
8. Provide loading, empty, and error states for dashboard data.

### 3.2 Success indicators

- An educator can identify high-risk students without reviewing the entire cohort manually.
- Each risk score includes understandable supporting signals.
- An educator can move from a warning signal to a student review or nudge.
- Protected APIs reject unauthenticated and unauthorized access.

## 4. Users and Roles

### 4.1 Educator

Educators are the primary users. They can:

- Sign in with email/password or Google OAuth.
- View cohort metrics and risk distribution.
- Search students by name, email, or signal.
- Filter by risk category and batch.
- Sort by highest risk, longest inactivity, or lowest quiz completion.
- Inspect student details, quiz activity, scores, deadlines, and signals.
- Send, schedule, and review outreach nudges.
- View reports, recent activity, engagement trends, and risk signals.

### 4.2 Administrator

Administrators use the same protected educator workflows and may access permitted educator data.

### 4.3 Student

Student-facing authentication and route scaffolding exist in the current project. A complete student portal with pending quizzes, quiz completion, and rich nudge interactions is future scope.

## 5. Risk Model

Each student receives a risk score from 0 to 100. A higher score indicates greater risk and should be treated as a prompt for educator review, not as a final judgment about the student.

### 5.1 Inputs

- `Q`: quiz completion rate from 0 to 100.
- `inactive_days`: full days since the student's last login.
- If there is no recorded login, inactivity is treated as 99 days for risk calculation.

### 5.2 Formula

```text
L = min(100, inactive_days x 25)
R = round(min(100, 0.6 x (100 - Q) + 0.4 x L))
```

Quiz completion contributes 60 percent of the score and inactivity contributes 40 percent. The inactivity component reaches its maximum after four days.

### 5.3 Risk categories

- **Healthy:** 0-39
- **Medium:** 40-69
- **High:** 70-100

The API also generates signals such as:

- Three or more days inactive: medium inactivity signal.
- Seven or more days inactive: high inactivity signal.
- Quiz completion below 50 percent: quiz signal.
- Low risk with zero inactive days: positive consistent-login signal.

### 5.4 Example

A student with 40 percent quiz completion and three inactive days receives:

```text
L = 3 x 25 = 75
R = 0.6 x (100 - 40) + 0.4 x 75 = 66
```

The student is classified as Medium risk and should be monitored or contacted.

## 6. Functional Requirements

### 6.1 Authentication and authorization

- The login page supports email/password login and Google OAuth through Auth.js.
- Protected application routes require an authenticated session.
- Student data APIs return `401 Unauthorized` when no valid session exists.
- Student data APIs return `403 Forbidden` when the user lacks educator or administrator access.
- Server-side authorization must use the verified session and must not trust a client-supplied user ID.
- Passwords must be stored as hashes, never as plain text.

### 6.2 Educator dashboard

The dashboard must display:

- Total students.
- Students at risk.
- High-risk students.
- Healthy students.
- Risk distribution.
- Engagement trend and recent activity where data is available.
- Early-warning signals with counts and recommended next steps.
- A student attention table.

The student table must display, where available:

- Name and email.
- Batch.
- Risk score and category.
- Quiz completion rate.
- Inactive days and last active time.
- Average score.
- Missed deadlines.
- Recommended action.

### 6.3 Search, filtering, and sorting

Educators must be able to:

- Search by student name or email.
- Search by generated signal text.
- Filter by Healthy, Medium, High, or all students.
- Filter by batch.
- Sort by risk score descending.
- Sort by inactivity descending.
- Sort by quiz completion ascending.

### 6.4 Student details

Selecting a student should expose the evidence used to prioritize them, including quiz attempts, completion rate, average score, missed deadlines, last login, notes, risk category, recommended action, and generated signals.

Persistent risk history is not currently stored in the database and is a future requirement.

### 6.5 Outreach and nudges

Educators must be able to:

- Send a check-in, early-warning, quiz-reminder, study-reminder, encouragement, follow-up, or custom message.
- Associate a nudge with a student and educator.
- Require a student response.
- Schedule a message for later delivery.
- Review message status, response status, timestamps, and message content.

The current implementation persists nudges in the database. Automated delivery and student responses beyond the current data model are future enhancements.

### 6.6 Reports and signals

The application should provide dedicated views for:

- Reports.
- Risk signals.
- Messages and outreach.
- Students.

These views should preserve the same authenticated educator context as the dashboard.

## 7. Data Model

The PostgreSQL database, accessed through Prisma, contains the following main entities:

- `users`: identity, role, account status, and authentication information.
- `educators`: educator profile and ownership of nudges.
- `students`: learner profile, batch, last login, and notes.
- `batches`: learner cohorts.
- `quizzes`: quizzes assigned to batches.
- `quiz_attempts`: student quiz status, score, and submission time.
- `student_activities`: timestamped activity records and optional JSON details.
- `nudges`: educator outreach, status, scheduling, and response fields.

The current risk score is calculated at read time from student login and quiz-attempt data. A separate persisted risk-score history table is not currently part of the implementation.

## 8. System Architecture

```text
Educator
        |
Next.js / React dashboard
        |
Protected API routes
        |
Authentication and authorization
        |
Prisma ORM + risk calculation
        |
PostgreSQL
```

The primary student endpoint reads database records, calculates quiz completion and inactivity, applies the risk formula, generates signals, and returns dashboard-ready data. The client dashboard consumes protected APIs and supports cached GET requests for the current browser tab.

## 10. Non-Functional Requirements

- Dashboard views should be responsive on desktop and mobile.
- Loading, successful empty, and error states must be distinguishable.
- API responses must not expose student data to unauthenticated users.
- Risk calculations must be deterministic and explainable.
- Secrets, database credentials, OAuth credentials, and tokens must not be committed.
- The application should support local development with seeded development data.

## 11. Current Scope and Limitations

The current release is an educator monitoring and outreach prototype. It does not yet provide:

- Persisted risk-history records and historical risk charts.
- A complete student portal with live pending quizzes and quiz completion.
- A machine-learning prediction model.
- Fully automated intervention delivery.
- Validated production thresholds based on historical retention outcomes.

The risk score is a prioritization aid. Educators should review the underlying signals and use professional judgment before taking action.

## 10. Project Scope for the First Version

The first version should focus only on the main workflow.

1. Add or display students.
2. Store quiz activity.
3. Calculate a risk score.
4. Show students on the educator dashboard.
5. Filter high-risk students.
6. Send a simple nudge.

## 13. Final Prototype Outcome

At the end of the project, the application should demonstrate this simple flow:

Student becomes inactive or misses quizzes
        ↓
Risk score increases
        ↓
Student appears as high risk on the Educator Dashboard
        ↓
Educator sends a nudge
        ↓
Student completes a quiz
        ↓
Risk score is recalculated
        ↓
Student's status improves

## 14. Core Idea

The main idea of this project is simple:

"Identify students who are becoming inactive and help educators take action before the student completely stops learning."

The project does not need advanced AI or complex infrastructure for the first version. A working dashboard, risk score calculation, student activity tracking, PostgreSQL database, and simple nudge system are enough for the first prototype.
